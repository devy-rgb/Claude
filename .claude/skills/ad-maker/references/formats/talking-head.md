# Format Module — Talking-Head / Call-In Show

One person to camera: a spokesperson, a founder, or a "caller" style testimonial.
TED-talk-meets-direct-response energy, or a phone-in show framing where the
speaker responds to an implied question. High authority, single credible voice.

**Engine stack:** hyper-real still (Nanobanana Pro) → Google Omni (default — its
16-block brief and subdued-delivery ability suit a measured spokesperson) or
Kling 3.0 (when you want the tightest lip-sync and native clip chaining).
Read `references/model-specs.md` and `references/production-discipline.md` first.

> **On Adsora Gen — the house default whenever `node tools/adsoragen.mjs --health`
> is LIVE (hard rule 15):** the on-camera avatar clip is a `t2v` / `i2v` clip with
> H3's **native** voice (lips locked to the sound; the accent is a prompt line).
> A **full read longer than 15 s is a chain of `i2v` clips** — clip 1 from a
> settled closed-lip portrait cut from the END of a clip (never mid-word), each
> next clip from the previous clip's last settled frame, one seed throughout,
> `-c copy` concat, joins sheet before sending. The exact procedure, measured
> costs (~$0.55 per ~47 s) and QA are in `references/talking-head-long-form.md`;
> `tools/talking-head-chain.sh` runs it from a chunks file. **Never build a long
> read on `avatar` (a supplied voice track):** it re-synthesizes the voice, muxes
> the original back, and the lips drift with length. The two AVATAR-LED defaults
> below are written for the Evolink/Seedance stack; on Adsora Gen, default 1 is
> met by H3's native audio, and default 2 (clone the avatar's own generated voice
> for the B-roll VO) still applies.

## AVATAR-LED AD DEFAULTS (REQUIRED — read first)

**Terminology.** This format is an **avatar-led ad**: an on-camera **avatar** (a
generated person) delivers the hook and the CTA to camera, with B-roll running
under a voiceover in between. The on-camera shots are **avatar clips**; the
in-between shots are **B-roll clips**. Same terms apply to `ugc-yapper` and any
face-to-camera format.

Two defaults are locked for every avatar-led ad. They hold on the **Evolink /
Seedance** stack (the configured default here); if a run uses Omni/Kling instead,
apply the same intent (best-quality model for the avatar clip, one cloned voice
throughout).

### 1. Avatar clips use the BEST Seedance model, not the fast one

- The **avatar clip** (the 5–15s on-camera shot where the avatar speaks) defaults
  to **`seedance-2.0-image-to-video`** — the full model, **NOT
  `seedance-2.0-fast-image-to-video`**. The fast model is fine for silent B-roll,
  but the avatar's face and lip-sync carry the ad, so the avatar clip gets the
  best model.
- Generate the avatar clip at a real length in the **5–15s** band (don't chop the
  hook into a 4s clip). `seedance-2.0` handles a single take in that range.
- **Only override on an explicit user instruction** ("use fast", "use Kling",
  "use Adsora Avatar", etc.). Absent that, avatar clip = `seedance-2.0-image-to-video`.
- B-roll clips may stay on `seedance-2.0-fast-image-to-video` to save cost.

### 2. One voice: clone the avatar's own voice with Chatterbox, use it everywhere

The avatar clip generated by `seedance-2.0` speaks with its own native voice and
matching lips. Make that the voice of the **entire** ad:

1. Generate the avatar clip(s) first (native audio on).
2. Extract that clip's audio and isolate the voice (Demucs `--two-stems=vocals`)
   → a clean mono 24 kHz ~7–12s **refAudio** of the avatar's voice.
3. **Clone it with Chatterbox** (`~/.adsora/vc-chatterbox`, see
   `references/faceless-video.md` §3) and synthesize the **B-roll voiceover** for
   every other beat with that cloned voice.
4. Keep the avatar clips' native audio (lips already match it); lay the cloned
   voice over the B-roll. Result: **one consistent voice across the whole ad**,
   matching the on-screen avatar, with real lip-sync on the avatar shots.

- Default VO engine for avatar-led ads is therefore **Chatterbox (clone), not a
  Kokoro preset.** Kokoro is the fallback only if the avatar clip has no usable
  audio to clone from.
- **Clone only the avatar's own generated voice.** Never clone the voice of a real
  person from the source competitor ad — that is impersonation and is out of
  bounds (see the "Original characters only" rule in Stage 3).

Config keys for both defaults live in `config/generators.json` → `routing`
(`avatar_formats`, `avatar_clip_model`, `avatar_clip_len_sec`, `avatar_voice`,
`avatar_voice_engine`).

## When this format fits the DNA
Authority / credentials · a contrarian reframe · a complex mechanism carried by
one confident voice · a strong single-result story. If the competitor ad was a
spokesperson piece or a founder monologue, this is the direct rebuild.

## Production rules

1. **One anchored character.** Full describe on the first still; CHARACTER LOCK on
   every later still. Default expression: warm, relaxed, direct — never tired.
2. **Eyeline:** straight to lens for a spokesperson/founder address (this format's
   default, unlike podcast). For a "call-in" framing, the speaker can glance
   slightly off-axis as if listening, then return to lens on the answer.
3. **Credibility fast.** If the DNA uses an authority stamp, it's one line early,
   then move on. Don't dwell.
4. **The reframe is the hinge.** If the competitor's belief shift runs through a
   "the real problem isn't X, it's Y" move, that clip gets the most performance
   attention.
5. **Deliberate pace.** Authority speaks with pauses. Brisk but not rushed. Emotion
   from micro-expressions and one open-hand beat that returns to rest, never big
   gestures or swaying.
6. **Mix B-roll in** — a lifestyle shot of the problem, a product B-roll. Never a
   pure talking-head sequence.
7. **Optional visible audience** (spokesperson-on-stage variant): script 1–2
   genuine reaction moments (a nod, note-taking) — never fake applause.
8. **Clip length:** hook 5–7s, talking 6–9s, B-roll 3–5s, CTA 4–6s. Split long
   lines at a natural pause; continuation clips start from the previous last frame.
9. **Voice:** one locked audio block, verbatim every clip. Accent in 3 places
   (delivery note, performance line, audio spec). If the script has a genuinely
   subdued beat, prefer Omni; on Kling, plan an ElevenLabs audio swap.
10. **Product** held in hand for product beats; label locked from a reference
    image or composited in post.

## Negative constraints (pool — pick 3–6 targeted per clip)

```
no phone visible anywhere in frame, no captions or on-screen text, speaker does
not drift position, camera angle does not change, one continuous take, locked-off
phone with only tiny natural drift, no dolly or push-in or zoom unless the beat
names it, mouth motion matches the dialogue exactly, no doubled or echoed words,
hands make at most one small beat and return to rest, no swaying, skin keeps
natural pores and texture, no beauty-filter smoothing, no studio strobe or ring
light, lighting does not flicker, product label stays readable and unwarped,
no AI-generated text on the label
```

## Paste-ready skeletons

### Still — master first frame (Frame A, NEW anchor, Nanobanana Pro)

```
Hyper realistic candid UGC phone-propped shot ONLY (no bokeh, no portrait mode):
A [age] [gender] [ethnicity] person with [build], [hair length/texture/colour
and how it behaves], [2–3 imperfections with exact location], wearing [garment,
colour, fit]. Seated (or standing) in [a real lived-in room / a clean
professional setting], with [2–3 named background objects], eye-level framing,
soft everyday light from [window/room] around [temp]K. Direct calm eye contact
to the lens, jaw relaxed, slight asymmetric closed-lip smile, chin level. Candid
mobile camera quality, natural colour, mild compression, slight sharpening.
Deep focus, no artificial blur. No phone visible anywhere in frame. No phone on
the counter. No captions. 9:16 vertical.
```

### Clip — spokesperson beat (Google Omni 16-block brief)

```
FORMAT: 8-second vertical 9:16 realistic UGC video.

QUALITY / FIDELITY LOCK: Match the reference image's lighting, texture, and phone
image quality exactly. Do not sharpen, do not enhance skin, no beauty filter, no
skin smoothing, no HDR, no clarity boost, no colour grade. Raw, organic, true to
the reference.

IDENTITY LOCK: Use @image1 as the exact identity. Preserve face structure, skin
tone, [hair], [marks], age, build, likeness exactly.

OUTFIT LOCK: [garment + colour + accessories]. Do not change the outfit.

ENVIRONMENT LOCK: Keep everything visible in @image1 — [background objects, wall,
surfaces]. Scene stays stable.

SCENE: [room], soft everyday [window] light, calm quiet atmosphere.

CAMERA: Locked-off phone perspective, eye-level, medium shot matching @image1's
framing. One continuous take. Tiny natural handheld drift only. No dolly,
push-in, pan, tilt, or zoom.

PERFORMANCE VIBE: Measured authority — a founder who has explained this a hundred
times and still means it. Calm, deliberate, warm. Small expressive face, one
light hand beat, no big motion.
PER-LINE: "[first sentence of the beat, EMPHASIS word in CAPS]" — steady eye
contact to lens, one slow blink, brows settle. "[second sentence]" — one open
hand lifts an inch on the key word and returns to rest, small nod at the end.

VOICE: [texture, age, rasp, pace] [accent] voice. Unpolished, not an announcer.

DIALOGUE: Speaking with accurate lip sync and natural pacing, the person says:
"[exact spoken lines for this beat, verbatim from 03-rebuild.md]"

TIMING: Line lands, half-second hold, clip ends on a completed word.

AUDIO / ENVIRONMENT SOUND: Realistic phone-recorded room audio — [ambient of the
room], voice close and real to the phone mic, not studio-polished. No music.

STYLE: Authentic TikTok/Reels UGC, raw smartphone footage, natural skin texture,
slightly imperfect realism.

NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS: No text overlays, captions, subtitles, logos, watermarks.
No VFX, CGI look, 3D, cartoon. No beauty filter, skin smoothing, HDR, colour
grade, artificial sharpening. No face morphing, identity drift, wardrobe change.
No unrequested camera movement. No extra fingers, warped hands, flicker, jitter.
Do not change the room. Mouth motion matches the dialogue; no doubled words.
```

### Kling variant of the same beat

Open `Realistic 9:16 counter-cam talking head, vertical phone framing.` then the
nine blocks as prose: shot anchor + grounding → opening event ("begins already
settled, mid-thought") → main action (one delivery) → state constraints → quoted
dialogue → performance decomposition → verbatim audio block with accent → 3–6
targeted negatives → continuity block only if a continuation. 1200–2000 chars.

## Assembly notes
Generate: Frame A → any B-roll / product stills → clips in cut order. Open on the
hook clip. Intercut a lifestyle B-roll after the problem beat and a product B-roll
before the CTA to break the talking head. Composite the product label in post.
Burn centered captions. CTA clip is the one place the speaker may look dead-center
to lens if they weren't already.

# HOUSE WORKFLOW — Call-In Advice Show variant

Full worked example: `references/house-workflows/call-in-advice-show.md`. Also
read `references/house-playbook.md`.

An on-camera host takes what plays like a **live call** from a person describing
a problem, and answers with blunt confident advice that introduces the product.
The caller is **heard only through the host's headset audio, never shown** — so
you get the authority of the advice-show genre without a second on-screen
character.

## Rules that differ from the base talking-head
1. **Host is an original character** (no real broadcasters). Ideally lock to an
   uploaded studio photo as `@image1` — an original person at a desk with
   headset + boom mic — and restate its full description as Identity/Outfit/
   Environment Lock in every clip (Omni has no memory).
2. **Studio camera, not phone.** "Static studio camera, locked-off, composed
   studio look, NOT a handheld phone shot." Keep the reference's exact blue
   backdrop glow / desk atmosphere.
3. **HOST SILENCE LOCK for the listening clip.** The clip where the caller talks
   and the host just listens: put a dedicated silence block *right after the
   scene description* (early = read first), repeat "mouth stays closed and still,
   no lip movement, no mouthing" at every performance beat, and add the override:
   "if the model would normally attach the spoken audio to the on-screen face by
   default, override that — the on-screen man stays completely silent and
   motionless at the mouth while the audio plays." Describe the caller as a
   **SECOND VOICE**: "heard through the headset audio feed, not lip-synced to any
   face on screen", thinner/compressed than the host's mic.
4. **Host eyeline sits just off to the side of the lens** ("as if listening and
   responding through his headset, not addressing the viewer") for the whole ad —
   he turns to the lens **once**, on the final CTA line only.
5. **Brand-safety softening (Omni "reputational risk" block).** An authoritative
   host endorsing a named product + clinical mechanism language trips Google's
   brand-safety classifier. Fixes: strip clinical/physiological mechanism
   language → plain wording ("helps your body wind down", not "calms your nervous
   system / pulls you out of fight or flight"). Remove genre-signalling words
   from the prompt text sent to the model ("on-air", "radio show", "call-in",
   "live", "caller") while keeping the visual concept (headset, desk, a
   conversation heard through an audio feed). Drop invented props not in the real
   reference (an "ON AIR" sign).
6. **Voice = a specific regional drawl**, locked verbatim across every clip the
   host speaks in (e.g. "soft Southern American drawl, warm and unhurried with
   easy rounded vowels, medium-low register, slight natural rasp").
7. **Clip 1**: host holds the same arms-crossed position as the reference for the
   whole clip — only small restrained movement (a small eyebrow lift, a subtle
   head tilt), no leaning, no uncrossing arms, no gesturing.

## Clip map (30–40s, 6 clips)
Hook (host reacts to the caller's situation) · Her problem (host silent,
listening) · Diagnosis (the reframe) · Mechanism + product pickup · Product name
+ reassurance · Close + caller's reply + CTA (host turns to lens for the first
time on the last line).

## Post
Caption text, a caller-ID style graphic, a "call ended" graphic → all added by
the skill in the assembly pass (`tools/assemble.mjs`: `captions`, `text`,
`overlays`), none baked into the prompt. Load a real product image as `@image2` before
generating the product-pickup clips or the placeholder label drifts.
