Myoguard Pro Pixar Whiteboard Ad — Seedance 2.5 Production Doc
Overview
Pixar-style 3D animated version of the live-action myoguard-whiteboard-ad, same script, same product, same disclosure requirement
Character: a chibi-proportioned Pixar-style animated version of the same "Dr. Sofia R. Marchetti, MD, PhD" persona, holding a wooden pointer stick
Format evolved twice during development: first as a fully pre-written board with only pointing (no writing), then pivoted to a progressive reveal where text and graphics appear on the board one at a time, in sync with the script, as she points to where each one appears
Narration: British English accent, precisely lip-synced dialogue throughout
Structure: 3 clips of ~15s each, frame-chained via first/last-frame anchoring plus voice-timbre-locked video references, same core technique as the live-action version
Disclosure: same dramatization/actor-portrayal line still applies and gets burned in during edit, even though the stylized cartoon format substantially lowers the risk of anyone mistaking this for a real physician

Shared Rules (apply across all three clips)
GAZE RULE:
She looks at the whiteboard specifically during the moment she is
pointing at a new element as it appears there, or re-pointing at an
existing one. During transitional words where she isn't pointing at
anything new, she looks back at the camera. She never points at empty,
unlabeled space.
 
GLOW RULE:
The battery icon and any existing checkmark have a soft glow as a
fixed, permanent part of their design, established once and never
changing. This glow does not pulse, brighten, dim, move, or transfer
to any other element at any point. No element other than the battery
icon and checkmarks ever glows. Nothing highlights or visually
emphasizes based on what she is currently saying, the only thing
indicating relevance is the stick itself.
 
STICK-TARGET RULE:
At every moment, the stick points only at the specific word or icon
named in that step, using an explicit spatial anchor (position
relative to another labeled element, e.g. "one text-line below X,
same left margin"), not a vague description like "the empty space
below X." The stick physically leads each new word into existence at
its tip, rather than text appearing first and the stick finding it
afterward. If a target sits low on the board, a pose reference image
showing the correct arm angle and height should be used, matching it
exactly for that beat, since text-only spatial descriptions were not
reliable enough on their own for lower board positions.
 
VOICE-CONTINUITY FIREWALL (Clips 2 and 3, referencing the previous
clip's video as a voice reference):
Use the previous clip ONLY as a vocal-timbre reference: match the
speaker's accent, pitch, and speaking rhythm. Do not reproduce,
repeat, or borrow any words, sentences, or dialogue content from it.
The dialogue for this clip is exclusively the text in the DIALOGUE
block and nothing else.

Troubleshooting Log
Format pivot: the project started as "everything already written, she only points," then changed to a progressive reveal where content appears as she talks about it, this was a deliberate creative change partway through, not a bug fix.
"GLP-1" was being pronounced with a V sound instead of a hard P → fixed with an explicit phonetic breakdown ("G-L-P-one, hard P, never V") in both the dialogue lock and as a standalone pronunciation note.
Irrelevant text and graphics were appearing on the board that weren't in the script for that clip → traced to using the complete whiteboard photo as a reference, its actual content was leaking through even when it was only meant to convey style. Fixed by removing that image from Clip 1 entirely and describing the target style in text instead, later solved more reliably with the last-frame anchoring technique below.
Introduced first/last-frame anchoring (generate a target image showing exactly what the board should look like at the end of each clip, then tell Seedance that image is the last frame) as a more reliable fix than describing the end state in prose. This is the same technique used on the live-action version.
Clip 3 initially needed a last-frame image, but a board-only last frame contradicted the product reveal that also needed to happen in that clip, since the reveal isn't a board state. Fixed once by generating a true last frame that included both the finished board and her holding the product. That version was then reconsidered and simplified: dropped the last-frame anchor for Clip 3 altogether, using only the complete whiteboard (style reference) and the product photo (reveal reference) as plain references instead, with the ending described directly in the timestep text.
The stick was pointing at the wrong text, or at empty space, when she mentioned "zinc carnosine" and "vitamins" → added the STICK-TARGET RULE with explicit relative spatial anchors ("one text-line below X, same left margin") instead of vague phrases like "the empty space below X."
Elements were glowing on the board that weren't supposed to (glow apparently being read as a dynamic "this is relevant now" highlight rather than a fixed property of specific icons) → added the GLOW RULE stating the glow belongs permanently to the battery icon and checkmarks only, never moves, and nothing else ever glows.
Even with explicit spatial anchors, the stick still pointed at the middle of the board instead of the bottom, where "ZINC CARNOSINE" and "80+ VITAMINS" actually sit → text-only spatial description wasn't reliable enough. Fixed by generating a dedicated pose-reference image showing the correct low arm angle and height, and instructing the model to match that image's pose exactly for those two beats. This is the strongest lesson from this project: for precise spatial placement, a visual pose reference outperforms a written spatial description.
